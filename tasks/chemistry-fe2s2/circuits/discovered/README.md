# discovered — melt-scheduled seven-frame block walk

This folder holds the discovered circuit for the `chemistry-fe2s2` task.
Its certified cost is 14,978 Toffolis and 126 peak qubits, a TQ-cost of
1,887,228. The accompanying paper (arXiv: TBD) describes it in the SI
section "Quantum chemistry simulation"; this README maps the construction
to the code. This README uses the names of the code: $\sigma$ is the comparand ($\tau$ in
the paper), and $f$ is the comparison bit ($h$ in the paper). The task
README maps the frame names `F`, `C`, `D`, `D2`, `s_thio`, `s_d45`, and
`x_muS` to the paper's notation.

An unstructured reversible table traversal forms one address control per
row, so coherent alias lookup and Pauli SELECT each approach one Toffoli
per table row. The melt-scheduled seven-frame block walk removes different
kinds of redundancy from those traversals. It chooses an equivalent alias
schedule whose nearby words repeat, and it orders Pauli masks into blocks
generated by commuting Clifford frames.

Repeated-span pruning reduces the alias traversal from 41,050 to 10,271
Toffolis. Frame blocking replaces the second dense traversal by a
3,327-Toffoli walk over 3,221 representatives plus 534 Toffolis of frame
apparatus. These choices give the following round trip.

```text
walk_step:
    declare i, σ, x                                  ▷ init
    keep, alt, θᵢ, θₐ ← pruned alias-tree walk at i  ▷ lookup
    f ← [σ < keep]                                   ▷ cmp
    swap i ↔ alt under f; alt now holds sel          ▷ cmp/select
    g ← interval flags for the frames active at sel  ▷ select-blk
    conjugate x by the frames selected by g          ▷ select-conj
    walk block roots at sel; apply masks and sign    ▷ select-pauli
    reverse the frame conjugations                   ▷ select-unconj
    clear g through the interval trie                ▷ select-unblk
    reverse that swap; i and alt come back           ▷ cmp/unselect
    f ← f ⊕ [σ < keep]                               ▷ cmp/rev
    mₖ ← X-measure the keep carriers                 ▷ unlookup/measure-keep
    mₐ ← X-measure the alternate scratch             ▷ unlookup/measure-alt
    W ← one-hot on address bits 2–5                  ▷ unlookup/window:prep
    repair the joint phase from mₖ, mₐ, and W        ▷ unlookup/window:fixup
    reverse the routing that prepared W              ▷ unlookup/window:unprep
```

Here `walk_step` names the round trip. $i$, $\sigma$, and $x$ are the
row, comparand, and system registers. $\mathrm{keep}$, $\mathrm{alt}$,
$\theta_i$, and $\theta_a$ are the addressed alias fields. $f$ is the
strict-comparison bit, and $\mathrm{sel}$ is the selected row. $g$ is the
seven-frame flag vector, and $m_k$ and $m_a$ are the two measurement
records. $W$ is a 16-slot window addressed by bits 2–5, because each
64-row window leaves its low two bits to Boolean phase planes.

The sections below describe the stages: “Melt scheduling and
repeated-span lookup,” “Commuting-frame block selection,” “Quadratic
frame controls,” and “Comparator and joint unlookup.” The schedules,
gauges, and hold policies are deterministic functions of the fixed
table. The build asserts every block's branch images and sign
polynomials row by row. At synthesis time, the circuit proves that each
frame's congruence network computes the same function as the phase form
it realizes. The evaluator checks the three boundary registers, the
global sign, and a clean workspace.

## Melt scheduling and repeated-span lookup (lookup)

Alias sampling represents a nonuniform discrete distribution using
equal-size buckets. Let $M=2^{16}$ be the capacity of each bucket. Bucket
$i$ assigns $\mathrm{keep}_i$ of its comparison values to term $i$ and
the remaining $M-\mathrm{keep}_i$ values to an alternate term
$\mathrm{alt}_i$. The number of comparison values selecting term $\ell$
is

$$
N_\ell
=
\mathrm{keep}_\ell
+
\sum_{i:\,\mathrm{alt}_i=\ell}
\left(M-\mathrm{keep}_i\right).
$$

The target quantized distribution fixes every $N_\ell$, but it does not
fix which underfilled bucket receives each contribution from a heavy term.
Reassigning those contributions while preserving the totals leaves the
represented distribution unchanged. This scheduling freedom therefore
adds no approximation error.

The scheduler spends that freedom on compressibility. It keeps
equal-coefficient light rows in aligned runs. A heavy row drains its
excess through whole runs before it moves to the next donation target.
Near-threshold rows pass remaining credit through aligned chains instead
of breaking a constant run. This draining process is the melt schedule.
In the spin-keyed part of the table, 18,463 of 20,374 adjacent pairs
consequently carry byte-identical alias words.

A reversible table walk descends an address tree and XORs leaf-dependent
data into carriers. With unrelated leaves, the active equality control
must change at each row. Updating that control requires a conjunction of
address literals, so the dense traversal pays one temporary AND per row,
apart from its endpoint convention. For this table that convention leaves
41,050 Toffolis.

A tree split is unnecessary when its left and right spans contain
identical in-domain word sequences. The distinguishing address bit cannot
affect the loaded word, so one child circuit implements both halves.
The graded range does not constrain values outside it, so the scheduler
may choose those values to complete such agreements. Pruning every provably
irrelevant split turns the melt schedule's repeated words into omitted
ANDs and leaves 10,271 Toffolis in the lookup traversal.

## Commuting-frame block selection (select-blk, select-pauli, select-unblk)

The second table walk selects a Pauli action rather than an alias word. A
dense implementation visits each row, forms its address-equality control,
and conditionally applies that row's bit flips and phase masks. The Pauli
gates themselves are Clifford, but changing the row control still needs
one temporary AND per row. The resulting dense SELECT traversal costs
41,050 Toffolis for this instance.

A frame is a fixed Clifford transformation that maps one Pauli mask to
another. If a representative operator is $P$ and a frame is $F$, a
matched row has the form $F P F^\dagger$ up to a known sign. A family of
commuting frames $F_1,\ldots,F_k$ generates the operators

$$
F_1^{b_1}\cdots F_k^{b_k}
P
F_k^{b_k}\cdots F_1^{b_1},
\qquad
b_j\in\{0,1\}.
$$

The bits $b_j$ select a subset of frames. Commutation makes the subset,
rather than an application order, the relevant label, so one
representative can stand for as many as $2^k$ rows. The table uses at
most five frames within one block and therefore places up to 32 rows
behind one block root.

Only the Pauli relation must be exact. The coefficient magnitude controls
how often the alias table selects a row, while SELECT acts on the Pauli
mask after that selection has occurred. Rows related by a frame may
therefore retain independent alias thresholds and alternate indices.
Their coefficient signs also remain independent, and the selected-sign
channel carries them. This separation allows exact mask matches from
approximate orbital symmetries without treating those symmetries as
equalities between Hamiltonian coefficients.

Seven commuting frames organize the table into 3,221 blocks across 14
contiguous regions. Address bits inside a block select its frame subset,
while interval flags identify which frames are active in the current
region. The Pauli traversal then visits block roots instead of individual
rows. It costs 3,327 Toffolis instead of the 41,050-Toffoli dense walk.
Constructing, applying, and clearing the shared frame controls costs
another 534.

## Quadratic frame controls (select-conj, select-unconj)

A real Clifford frame acts on a computational basis state by a linear
transformation of its bits. It also applies a sign described by a
quadratic Boolean form $q(x)$. Controlling the frame by a flag $g$ controls that sign
through the cubic expression $gq(x)$. Directly expanding the quadratic
form can produce many redundant controlled products.

The polarization of $q$ defines a binary matrix $B$ through

$$
q(u+v)+q(u)+q(v)=u^{\mathsf T}Bv.
$$

Over $\mathrm{GF}(2)$ this matrix is alternating, so its rank is even.
Write $\operatorname{rank}(B)=2r$. An invertible linear change of
coordinates reduces the quadratic part to $r$ independent products,

$$
q(x)=y_1y_2+y_3y_4+\cdots+y_{2r-1}y_{2r}
+\text{linear terms}.
$$

The coordinate change and its inverse cost only CNOTs and are therefore
Toffoli-free. Each controlled product $g y_{2j-1}y_{2j}$ needs one CCZ,
while controlled linear terms need only CZs and are also Toffoli-free.
Conversely, one CCZ contributes polar rank at most two, so no realization
can use fewer than $r$ CCZs. The controlled quadratic phase thus needs
exactly $\operatorname{rank}(B)/2$ CCZs behind the CNOT congruence
network.

Minimal-rank coordinates reduce the $X\leftrightarrow Y$ frame `C` from
19 to 10 CCZs in each spin sector. The iron-site reflection `D` uses 10,
the composed reflection `D2` uses 6, and the three orbital swaps use 2,
2, and 1. Synthesis proves these
forms equivalent to the original frame actions on all system bits.

A Pauli representative also admits a Z-gauge choice that redistributes
known sign corrections between the representative and its frames. A
deterministic search in `gen_constants.py` over the complete table
chooses the gauge masks by their exact sign-channel Toffoli cost. It reduces
that channel from 769 Toffolis without a gauge to 117. A second search
places seven recurring sign monomials on existing block-walk node
controls. The walk already pays for those controls, so the emitted
channel drops to 108 Toffolis. Those placements are Toffoli savings,
not changes to the selected Pauli signs.

The frame flags apply only on their valid address intervals. Fourteen
intervals share borrow prefixes through a trie keyed by address bit and
prefix, rather than recomputing every boundary comparison independently.
The same accumulated flags control the forward frame applications and
their reversals, so the circuit pays for the interval logic once in each
direction.

## Comparator and joint unlookup (cmp, cmp/select, cmp/unselect, cmp/rev, unlookup/measure-keep, unlookup/measure-alt, unlookup/window:prep, unlookup/window:fixup, unlookup/window:unprep)

A borrow chain over the 16 bit positions evaluates a strict
comparison. At each position the next borrow depends nonlinearly on the
incoming borrow and the two current bits. Computing that conjunction as
a temporary AND costs one Toffoli, and measurement releases it without
another Toffoli. All 16 borrow stages can therefore remain temporary
rather than requiring a persistent ripple workspace.

The first comparator produces $f$. A controlled bitwise swap then places
the selected index in the bank consumed by the frame walk while
preserving the other branch. The swap costs 16 Toffolis because each of
its 16 bit positions needs one controlled exchange. After SELECT, an
identical swap restores the two index banks, and the same strict
comparator is XORed into $f$ to clear it. The two comparator passes and
two swaps together cost 64 Toffolis.

The second comparator requires the original threshold, so the keep bank
must remain live throughout SELECT. Erasing it earlier would destroy the
value needed to clear $f$ and force another threshold lookup. The
alternate-index scratch likewise remains available until the circuit
reverses the controlled selection.

Measurement-based uncomputation removes a data bank without reversing its
lookup, but it does not erase the lookup's effect for nothing. If the
bank holds the address-dependent string $d(i)$ and its X-basis measurement
returns $m$, the address amplitude acquires the phase

$$
(-1)^{m\cdot d(i)}.
$$

That phase varies with $i$, and the circuit must correct it. Measuring the keep and
alternate banks separately produces two such Boolean phase functions,
but both depend on the same row address. Their exponents can be XORed
classically and synthesized by one address traversal rather than two.

The joint repair walks 642 windows of 64 rows. Within a window, address
bits 2–5 select one of 16 one-hot slots. Boolean planes in the low
two bits encode the remaining variation as Reed–Muller expansions.
Preparing the one-hot routing costs 15 Toffolis, and the joint fixup
costs 767. Once its nonlinear planes are available, outcome-conditioned
CZs apply the remaining corrections and are Toffoli-free.

## Workspace ceilings and hold policies (lookup, cmp, select-blk, select-unblk, unlookup/window:fixup)

A table walk repeatedly faces a time-space choice. It can keep a path
conjunction alive for reuse by both children, which spends a qubit and
saves its reconstruction. Or it can erase the conjunction and pay another
Toffoli when the sibling needs the same control. A shared split wire and an accumulator form
provide intermediate choices between those extremes.

At circuit-build time, an exact dynamic program evaluates those choices
at every node of the fixed lookup and repair trees. Its state records the
node, the available hold budget, and the form of the incoming control. It
minimizes the exact temporary-AND count rather than applying one uniform
retention rule to the whole tree. The lookup gets a hold budget of
22 but saturates at 15 live holds, so additional allowance cannot remove
another Toffoli. The repair walk runs the same dynamic program under a
hold budget of 38 but saturates at 11 live holds.

The largest live set occurs during frame-flag teardown rather than during
the much larger lookup traversal. At that point it contains 72 boundary
wires: 16 for $i$, 16 for $\sigma$, and 40 for $x$. It also holds
16 keep carriers, 16 selection wires, the comparison bit, seven
frame flags, and up to 14 transient trie wires. The keep carriers cause
the apparent anomaly: they must survive the whole frame walk so the
second comparator can erase $f$ against the original threshold.

Every other walk's hold budget equals its headroom below that
frame-imposed qubit ceiling of 126. The exact dynamic program uses a hold
only where it saves a Toffoli. The lookup therefore stops after its 15
useful holds, and the repair stops after its 11 useful holds. No held
wire raises the peak above the workspace that the frame walk already forces.
